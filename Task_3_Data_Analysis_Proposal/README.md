
# Task 3: Propose Data Analysis Approaches

## Overview

The third task focused on proposing data analysis approaches that could be used to analyse Commonwealth Bank social media data.

The purpose was to explain how social media data could be analysed to generate useful business insights and how these insights could support decision-making.

The proposal focused on areas such as customer sentiment, engagement, topics, trends, customer pain points, and response prioritisation.

## Background

Social media provides organisations with large amounts of customer-generated information.

Customers can use social media to:

- Ask questions
- Report problems
- Provide feedback
- Share positive experiences
- Report scams or suspicious activity
- Comment on products and services
- Respond to campaigns and communications

Analysing this information can help an organisation better understand customer experiences and identify areas that may require attention.

## Objectives

The main objectives of the proposed analysis were to:

- Identify common topics discussed by customers.
- Understand customer sentiment.
- Measure social media engagement.
- Identify trends over time.
- Identify customer pain points.
- Prioritise posts that require a response.
- Combine social media information with existing business data.
- Generate insights that can support business decisions.

## 1. Topic and Theme Analysis

Topic analysis can be used to identify the subjects that customers discuss most frequently.

Possible topics include:

- Digital Banking
- Fraud and Scams
- Financial Wellbeing
- Security Education
- Card Services
- Customer Service
- Banking Products
- Payments

The analysis can measure the number of posts associated with each topic and compare engagement across topics.

### Example Findings

In the provided social media dataset:

- Digital Banking was one of the most frequently discussed topics, with 13 posts.
- Fraud and Scams appeared in 11 posts.
- Financial Wellbeing appeared in 9 posts.

This type of analysis can help identify the issues and subjects receiving the most attention from customers.

## 2. Sentiment Analysis

Sentiment analysis can be used to determine whether customer posts express positive, neutral, negative, or mixed sentiment.

The dataset contained:

- 26 positive posts
- 15 neutral posts
- 11 negative posts
- 8 mixed posts

Sentiment analysis could help identify:

- Positive customer experiences
- Customer dissatisfaction
- Complaints
- Concerns about products or services
- Potential reputation issues

Negative sentiment could then be investigated further to understand the underlying customer issue.

## 3. Engagement Analysis

Engagement analysis can be used to measure how customers interact with social media posts.

Possible engagement measures include:

- Likes
- Replies
- Reposts
- Views
- Link clicks
- Total engagement
- Engagement rate

The analysis can identify posts that receive unusually high levels of engagement.

For example, post `SM048`, which discussed protecting customers from "safe account" scams, recorded 12,165 total engagements.

This indicates that fraud and security-related content can generate significant customer interest.

## 4. Trend Analysis

Trend analysis can be used to identify how customer discussions and engagement change over time.

The analysis could examine:

- Changes in the number of posts.
- Changes in customer sentiment.
- Changes in engagement.
- Growth in discussions around particular topics.
- Changes in customer concerns.

Time-based analysis could help identify emerging issues and determine whether particular topics become more important during specific periods.

## 5. Customer Pain-Point Analysis

Social media data can be used to identify customer problems and recurring complaints.

Customer intent can be used to classify posts into categories such as:

- Questions
- Complaints
- Feature feedback
- Praise
- Scam reports
- Educational requests
- Product updates
- Campaign responses

Analysing these categories can help identify common customer pain points.

For example, repeated complaints about a particular digital banking feature could indicate an opportunity to improve the customer experience.

## 6. Customer Response Prioritisation

Not every social media post requires the same level of attention.

A response-prioritisation approach could consider:

- Negative sentiment
- Customer complaints
- Scam reports
- Questions requiring assistance
- High engagement
- Potential reputational risk

In the dataset, 11 out of 60 posts were flagged as requiring a response.

Posts with both high engagement and negative sentiment could receive additional attention because they may affect a larger audience.

## 7. Combining Social Media with Existing Business Data

Social media data can become more valuable when combined with existing structured business data.

For example, social media information could potentially be combined with:

- Customer service records
- Product information
- Transaction data
- Complaint records
- Fraud reports
- Customer demographics
- Marketing campaign data
- Website activity

This could help the organisation compare what customers are saying online with actual business activity.

For example, if social media shows an increase in complaints about a banking service, customer service records could be analysed to determine whether there is also an increase in related support requests.

## 8. Business Applications

The proposed analysis approaches could support several business functions.

### Marketing

Social media analysis can help identify:

- Popular topics
- Successful campaigns
- Customer interests
- Content generating high engagement

### Customer Service

The analysis can help identify:

- Common questions
- Complaints
- Customer pain points
- Posts requiring responses

### Fraud Prevention

Social media discussions about scams and suspicious activity can help identify emerging fraud concerns.

This information could potentially complement existing fraud monitoring processes.

### Product Development

Customer feedback can highlight:

- Product problems
- Feature requests
- Usability issues
- Areas for improvement

### Brand Monitoring

Sentiment and engagement analysis can help monitor how customers respond to the organisation and its communications.

### Strategic Decision-Making

Combining social media insights with existing business data can provide a broader view of customer behaviour and business performance.

## Proposed Analysis Process

The overall analytical process could follow these stages:

1. Collect social media data.
2. Clean and prepare the data.
3. Classify posts by topic and sentiment.
4. Analyse engagement metrics.
5. Identify trends over time.
6. Identify customer pain points.
7. Prioritise posts requiring attention.
8. Combine social media data with existing business data.
9. Generate business insights.
10. Use the insights to support decision-making.

## Key Insights from the Dataset

The analysis of the provided dataset demonstrated several useful patterns:

- Digital Banking was the most frequently represented topic with 13 posts.
- Fraud and Scams appeared in 11 posts.
- Financial Wellbeing appeared in 9 posts.
- Positive sentiment was the largest sentiment category with 26 posts.
- Fraud and security-related content generated substantial engagement.
- Post `SM048` recorded 12,165 total engagements.
- 11 of the 60 posts were flagged as requiring a response.
- Views and total engagement showed a strong relationship in the dataset.

These findings demonstrate how social media data can provide information about customer interests, concerns, engagement, and potential business issues.

Social media data can provide valuable information about customer experiences, interests, concerns, and interactions with Commonwealth Bank.
